# Problem Statement

## Background

In modern semiconductor fabrication facilities producing advanced 3nm and 5nm integrated circuits, chips are manufactured using highly sophisticated process technologies involving Extreme Ultraviolet (EUV) lithography, Reactive Ion Etching (RIE), Atomic Layer Deposition (ALD), and Chemical Mechanical Planarization (CMP).

A single wafer passes through hundreds of tightly controlled processing steps. Even small variations in equipment parameters such as chamber pressure, RF power, or process temperature can significantly affect final wafer yield.

## The Problem

At advanced semiconductor nodes, a 1% drop in wafer yield can result in losses worth millions of dollars. Root causes often remain hidden across thousands of equipment sensor readings, process parameters, and defect inspection images.

Process engineers spend days or even weeks manually analyzing logs, sensor records, and defect maps to determine which equipment parameter caused the yield loss. During this delay, additional wafer lots may continue processing under faulty conditions, increasing scrap and production losses.

## Who is Affected

### Yield Enhancement Engineers (YEE)

Responsible for identifying production issues and improving wafer yield.

### Process Integration Engineers (PIE)

Analyze interactions between multiple fabrication steps and process modules.

### Fab Process Engineers

Manage lithography, etch, deposition, and CMP tools and must quickly identify equipment abnormalities.

### Manufacturing Operations Teams

Need rapid risk assessment for upcoming production batches before processing begins.

## Why It Matters

- Yield loss directly impacts semiconductor manufacturing profitability.
- Delays in root-cause identification increase production scrap.
- Equipment faults may remain undetected for multiple production lots.
- Advanced-node wafer lots can represent extremely high manufacturing value.
- Fast and accurate diagnostics can significantly improve fab productivity and profitability.

## Why Existing Solutions Fall Short

### Traditional SPC Systems

Most Statistical Process Control (SPC) systems monitor individual parameters independently and fail to identify complex interactions between multiple variables.

### Manual Investigation

Engineers often rely on spreadsheets, charts, and disconnected data sources, making diagnosis time-consuming.

### Reactive Workflows

Most systems identify problems only after yield loss has already occurred instead of preventing future production risk.

### Limited Intelligence

Current tools provide monitoring and visualization but generally do not perform automated root-cause attribution or predictive batch risk assessment.
